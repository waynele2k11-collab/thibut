Technical Standards

Defining the rigorous specifications required to bridge traditional calligraphy craftsmanship with verifiable digital provenance. These standards ensure structural integrity across both physical and digital manifestations.

science I. Archival Materials

SUMI-E GRADE

Carbon Particulate Density

The formulation must achieve a lightfastness rating of Class I (ASTM D4303) ensuring zero degradation under standard museum lighting conditions over 200 years.

  • Viscosity 12-15 cP
  • pH Level 7.2 - 7.5 (Neutral)
SUBSTRATE

Washi Integration

100% Kozo fiber structure required for optimal ink absorption and tensile strength during the wet mounting process.

camera_enhance II. Digital Capture Protocols

Photogrammetry & Scanning

To accurately translate the depth of ink absorption and the subtle topography of the washi, standard flatbed scanning is insufficient. We employ multi-angle raking light capture to create a topographical map of each brush stroke.

wb_incandescent

Illumination Array

Cross-polarized lighting at 15°, 45°, and 75° incidence angles.

aspect_ratio

Resolution Base

Minimum 1200 ppi optical capture, yielding 16-bit uncompressed TIFF formats.

palette

Color Calibration

Strict adherence to Adobe RGB (1998) color space utilizing custom X-Rite profiling per session.

fingerprint III. The Digital Twin & Provenance

1

Physical Hanko

The artwork is finalized with a traditional Cinnabar paste seal. The micro-imperfections in the hand-carved stone and the viscosity of the paste create a unique, unforgeable physical signature.

2

Cryptographic Hash

The high-resolution scan of the Hanko seal is analyzed. A cryptographic hash (SHA-256) is generated based on the specific topographic mapping of the red pigment distribution.

3

On-Chain Registry

The hash, along with the artwork metadata and high-res capture, is minted to an immutable ledger, establishing an unbroken link between the physical object and its digital provenance.
