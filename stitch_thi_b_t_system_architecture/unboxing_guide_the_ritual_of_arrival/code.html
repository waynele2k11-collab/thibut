The Unveiling

The arrival of a Thi Bút piece is not merely a delivery; it is the beginning of a relationship between collector and creator. Approach the unboxing as a ritual of anticipation.

Preparation of Space

Before breaking the seal, prepare the environment. A serene space honors the craftsmanship contained within.

  • lightbulb Lighting: Seek soft, indirect natural light. Avoid harsh artificial overheads that may distort the subtle variations in the sumi ink.
  • air Atmosphere: Ensure the room is climate-controlled. Sudden shifts in humidity or temperature can stress the delicate washi paper.
  • space_dashboard Ma (Negative Space): Clear a large, flat surface. The concept of 'Ma'—intentional emptiness—allows the artwork to breathe as it is revealed.

The Opening

1

The Cord

Carefully untie the Sanada-himo cord. This traditional woven cotton tie has secured precious objects for centuries. Slip it off rather than pulling.

2

The Kiri Lid

Lift the Paulownia wood lid straight up to avoid friction. The wood is naturally porous, designed to protect the scroll from humidity.

3

The Washi

Wash and dry hands thoroughly before touching the internal Washi paper wrapping. Natural oils can transfer over time.

4

The Scroll

Grasp the wooden jiku (roller ends). Gently unroll the artwork upon your prepared surface, allowing it to relax naturally.

Care & Preservation

True appreciation lies in enduring care. A traditional scroll is a living document; its materials interact with the environment over decades.

Light & Climate

Strictly avoid direct sunlight, which will bleach the ink and embrittle the silk mounting. Maintain a moderate, stable climate. Prolonged exposure to high humidity invites mold, while extreme dryness causes cracking.

Seasonal Rotation

Adopt the practice of rotating displayed works. A scroll should rest in its Kiri box for several months a year to recover from environmental stress and gravity. This cyclic display enhances the joy of viewing.
